Math · MA.6.AR.2.1 I Do

Drive 1: Solving for the Missing Points

The Eagles have scored x points so far and need 6 more to reach 20 total. That's written as a one-step equation:

x + 6 = 20

To isolate x, undo the "+6" by subtracting 6 from BOTH sides: x + 6 − 6 = 20 − 6, so x = 14.

Test Tactic

Whatever operation is attached to the variable, do the OPPOSITE operation to both sides. Addition undoes with subtraction; subtraction undoes with addition.

Drive 2: Solving for Yards Per Catch

Jalen threw for a total of 100 yards on 4 completions. If y is the average yards per completion:

4y = 100

To isolate y, undo the multiplication by dividing BOTH sides by 4: 4y ÷ 4 = 100 ÷ 4, so y = 25.

Test Tactic

Multiplication undoes with division; division undoes with multiplication — the same "opposite operation" rule as addition and subtraction.

Drive 4: Reading the Whole Box Score

A real box score mixes addition/subtraction and multiplication/division equations together. FAST tests whether you can tell which "opposite operation" a given equation needs.

"x + [number] = [number]" or "x − [number] = [number]" → undo with the opposite of + or −.

"[number]y = [number]" or "y ÷ [number] = [number]" → undo with the opposite of × or ÷.

Test Tactic

Look at what's directly attached to the variable — that single operation tells you exactly what to undo, and how.

Playoff Push

Three minutes left, ball on the 40-yard line, and the Eagles trail by four. Coach Whitfield calls the same play he called in Week 1 — but this time, everyone in the huddle knows exactly what's coming.

Marcus lines up wide, the same route he dropped back in September. Jalen takes the snap, looks Marcus's way for half a second, then checks down to a different receiver entirely. Marcus doesn't flinch. He just runs the next route, full speed, like it was never in question.

Two plays later, it's Marcus's number called again. This time he catches it clean, spins away from a tackle, and picks up the first down that keeps the drive alive. On the sideline, Coach Whitfield doesn't say a word. He doesn't need to.
